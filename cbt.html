Therapy approaches

Cognitive behavioural

Thoughts, feelings, body and actions all affect each other. Change one, and the others shift. CBT-informed OT works mostly on the action side: experiments, steps and routines.

Aaron Beck's idea, simply

The CBT cycle

Something happens. A thought pops up. It shapes how we feel, what the body does, and what we do next. What we do then feeds the next situation.

SituationSomething happens
Thought"I can't do this"
FeelingAnxious, 80/100
BodyTight chest, fast heart
ActionAvoid it

Avoiding it meansthe thought never gets tested

Scope. Estus OTs use these as informed approaches inside OT goals: daily routines, participation and doing what matters. We are not delivering psychological therapy. If someone needs treatment for a mental health condition, we refer to a psychologist and work alongside them.

How OTs use it

CBT-informed tools in OT

Behavioural experiments

Test a prediction in real life. "If I ask for help, they'll be annoyed." Try it, then compare.

Graded exposure

Climb a ladder of small steps towards something feared. Driving, shops, phone calls.

Activity scheduling

Plan activities that bring mastery and enjoyment. Action first, motivation follows.

Problem solving

Name the problem, list options, pick one, try it, review.

Thought records

Slow a hot thought down on paper. Useful for older teens and adults who like structure.

Coping cards

A short card with the plan for a hard moment. Carry it into the situation.

Thinking traps

Useful names for common patterns. Use them gently, and check whether the thought is actually accurate first.

All or nothing

"If it's not perfect, it's a failure."

Catastrophising

"If I get lost, it'll be a disaster."

Mind reading

"They think I'm weird."

Fortune telling

"I'll fail the test."

Should statements

"I should be able to do this by now."

Discounting the good

"That went well, but it was luck."

The Estus way

CBT, neuroaffirming

Standard CBT can treat accurate thoughts as errors. Many neurodivergent people have been judged, excluded or misunderstood for real.

Do

  • Check if it's accurate"People will judge me" may be based on real experience. Validate first, then look at what helps.
  • Remember double empathyMisunderstandings between autistic and non-autistic people go both ways. It's not one person's thinking error.
  • Make it concreteVisuals, written steps, scales and examples. Special interests can carry the ideas.
  • Build feelings words firstIf naming feelings is hard, start with body signals and a simple scale.

Don't

  • Call real barriers distortionsAn inaccessible environment needs changing, not reframing.
  • Rely on talk aloneDoing beats discussing. Experiments teach more than debates.
  • Push exposure to sensory painExposure is for fear, not for sensory input that actually hurts.
  • Rush the ladderThe client sets the pace. Staying on a step is progress.
