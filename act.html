Acceptance and commitment

Why it fits

Why ACT belongs in OT

Values drive doing

OT is about meaningful occupation. ACT starts with what matters to the person, then builds action from there.

Not about removing feelings

We don't wait for anxiety to go before acting. Clients learn to carry it and still take part.

Small steps count

Committed action is a Small Hinges experiment by another name. One doable step, then the next.

Scope. Estus OTs use these as informed approaches inside OT goals: daily routines, participation and doing what matters. We are not delivering psychological therapy. If someone needs treatment for a mental health condition, we refer to a psychologist and work alongside them.

The hexaflex

The six core processes

Six skills in three pairs. Together they make up psychological flexibility.

Open up

Acceptance

Making room for hard feelings instead of fighting them.

  • OT example: noticing the anxiety before the shops and going anyway, with headphones.
  • Say: "Can you let that feeling be there, and still take the next step?"
Open up

Defusion

Seeing thoughts as thoughts, not orders or facts.

  • OT example: "I'm having the thought that I'll fail" before a driving lesson.
  • Say: "What story is your mind telling you right now?"
Be present

Present moment

Noticing what's here now, inside and out.

  • OT example: noticing body signals before a meltdown.
  • Say: "What do you notice in your body right now?"
Be present

Self-as-context

You are more than your thoughts, feelings and labels.

  • OT example: "I'm someone who struggles with mornings", not "I'm lazy".
  • Say: "Who's the you that notices all this?"
Do what matters

Values

What matters to you. The direction, not the destination.

  • OT example: "Being a good friend" behind wanting to join the server.
  • Say: "What would you want this to be about?"
Do what matters

Committed action

Small, doable steps towards values, even when it's hard.

  • OT example: one Small Hinges experiment for a week.
  • Say: "What's one small step you could take this week?"

The choice point

Russ Harris's choice point is an easy way to show ACT on one page. Hard stuff shows up. We choose a move.

Choice pointI notice. I choose.
Towards moveCloser to what matters
Away moveEscape or avoid for now
Away moves aren't bad. Sometimes leaving is the right call, especially for sensory overload. The question is: does this move take me where I want to go, over time?

The Estus way

ACT, neuroaffirming

ACT can be used to push people into coping with things that shouldn't be put up with. We don't do that.

Do

  • Accept feelings, not harmAcceptance is for inner experience. Inaccessible places and unfair treatment get changed, not accepted.
  • Use their valuesValues come from them, not from neurotypical "shoulds". Special interests are often a rich source.
  • Make metaphors concreteUse pictures, objects and games. Literal thinkers may find abstract metaphors confusing.
  • Let stims helpStimming can be a way of making room for big feelings. It's welcome.

Don't

  • Use ACT to build masking"Accept the discomfort and act normal" is not ACT. It's harm.
  • Rush to actionWithout values, committed action is just compliance.
  • Debate thoughtsIn ACT we don't argue whether a thought is true. We ask if it's helpful.
  • Treat away moves as failuresRest and retreat are sometimes exactly right.
