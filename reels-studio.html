01 · How it works

Shoot once, prompt, check, post

Claude does the edit in its own workspace: it cuts the footage, draws every graphic in code in our fonts and colours, makes the sound, and hands back a finished MP4. Your job is the clip, the hook and the final check.

1ShootOne vertical clip, 20 to 30 seconds, no talking. A few different looks. See Shoot.
2Pick the hookTake a card from the Must Be Nice bank. The card already has the line, the cause, the hinge and the caption.
3PromptAttach the clip in Claude and paste a prompt from the builder. Name the format, the CTA and the end screen.
4Check and fixWatch it twice: once on mute, once with sound. Ask for fixes in plain words. "Slower, I can't read it" works.
5PostRun the checklist, paste the caption, add a sound in the app if you want one, post.

What Claude already knows

  • The Must Be Nice series, its four beats and the card bank, from past chats and this hub.
  • Our brand: Oswald, Barlow and Barlow Semi Condensed, navy, blue, teal, plum, ink and paper.
  • The end screens we built, saved as Design artifacts it can reopen.
  • Our rules: no em-dashes, no diagnoses named in ads, Ahpra limits.

What to tell it every time

  • The clip, attached to the message.
  • The hook, word for word, or the card name.
  • The format and roughly how long.
  • The CTA and which end screen.
  • Anything that must not change ("keep the 3am timing").

Speed tip. Ask for "six stills first" before the full render. You see the layout in a minute and only render once it's right.

05 · End screens and CTA

Finish on one clear next step

The end screen is where the Reel earns its keep. Four are built. Name the one you want, or ask Claude for a new one.

Anatomy of a strong end screen

123456
  1. Callback. "Must be nice." struck through. Ties it to the series in half a second.
  2. The flip. "Could be you." Big, short, hopeful. Never a promise.
  3. The why. One line: the turn ("Their setup fits their brain") or the Small Hinge it matches.
  4. One button. Verb first, who they meet, where to go: "Book a chat with an Estus OT. Link in bio." Pulse ring and a finger tap draw the eye.
  5. Trust chips. Telehealth Australia-wide. NDIS + private. Facts only.
  6. Brand. Four colour band and the wordmark. Then hold the finished screen for 3 seconds or more.

Makes a CTA stronger
  • One action only
  • Starts with a verb: Book, Follow, Tap
  • Says who they meet: an Estus OT
  • Names the Small Hinge when the hook matches one
  • "Free" only if the first chat really is free
  • On screen 3 seconds or more after it lands
Never
  • Two asks at once ("Follow and book and DM us")
  • Fake urgency or made-up spot counts
  • Outcomes or timeframes ("sleep better in 2 weeks")
  • Testimonials or reviews (Ahpra)
  • Naming a diagnosis ("Got ADHD?") in ads
  • A link in the caption on Instagram: it isn't clickable
06 · Reading pace

If they can't read it, it didn't happen

Our first 3am cut flew through its cards in under a second each. Nobody could read them. The slower cut runs 28 seconds and works. Rule: 1 second to notice, plus 1 second for every 3 words. Never under 1.5 seconds.

Hold it on screen for at least
3.7seconds

8 words

0.2 to 0.5sThe transition move itself. Fast is slick.
1.7s +A title card ("11:47") after it lands. Longer if it has a line under it.
3s +The finished end screen, after the last element lands.

How long should the whole thing be?

TypeLengthWhy
Text walk, deadpan stare12 to 16sTwo or three beats, then the end screen. Short enough to loop.
Motion graphics (tabs, moodboard)15 to 20sThe build-up needs time to land, then the relief.
Multi-scene with transitions20 to 30sEach scene needs its own reading time. Cut a scene before you speed up.

09 · Shoot

Two minutes of filming, five Reels

Film vertical, eye level, chest up, no talking. Walk slowly through the house or office and give the camera these looks. Deadpan is the whole joke: the text says it, your face doesn't react.

1
Dead stareStraight down the lens, two or three seconds. The hook frame.
2
Glance awayLook off to the side like you heard something. Searching, replaying.
3
Look downFlat, tired, defeated. The "face drops" beat.
4
Lean inCome close to the lens and hold. The punchline.
5
Head turnTurn away and back. Great for a whip or a reveal.
6
Walk pastWalk toward and past the camera. Loops well.
Do
  • Daylight from a window in front of you
  • Leave space below the chin for text
  • Plain top, one strong colour
  • Shoot 20 to 30 seconds, even for a 12 second Reel
  • Hold the phone steady with two hands, or use a stand
Avoid
  • Big brand logos on clothes
  • Clients, client homes or anything with client details
  • Other staff on camera without asking them
  • Talking. The format works on mute
  • Filming horizontal

Sound effects in, music in the app

Claude makes every sound effect from scratch and times it to the picture, so there's nothing to licence. Music is different: add it inside Instagram or TikTok from their own library.

Tap and typingFake taps, address bar typing.

Adding music. Business accounts only get the commercially cleared music in the app's library, so pick from that. Keep the Reel's own sound at about half so the effects still land, or ask Claude for a silent version.

11 · Our rules

Kind, honest, ours

Same rules as the rest of our marketing. See Must Be Nice for the series rules and Meta Ads System for ads.

TopicDoDon't
The momentName a moment everyone recognises: keys, 3am, the group chat.Name a condition, or ask "do you have ADHD?" (Meta personal attributes rule).
The hookOne complete sentence, on screen from frame one, one block, one size, one colour. "Parents we work with often say it must be nice when..." all in the same sentence."Must be nice" alone with the rest in another colour, a hook split into beats, or a small lead-in line above it.
ToneDeadpan, warm, a bit funny. Laugh with, never at.Mock, shame or pathologise anyone. No "fix", "normal", "suffers".
ClaimsSay what we work on and how. "One area, one session."Promise outcomes or timeframes. No testimonials or reviews (Ahpra).
PicturesOur footage and pictures Claude draws.Pinterest, Google Images, other people's posts, stock with a watermark.
PeopleStaff who have said yes.Clients, ever. Anyone who hasn't agreed.
WritingPlain words, short lines, Australian spelling.Em-dashes, jargon, more than one CTA.
Sign-offA clinician reads the card copy and caption before it goes out.Post copy nobody clinical has read.
How Claude does it, for the curious
  • It works in a private workspace with ffmpeg (cutting, grading, encoding) and a headless browser that draws each frame from HTML and CSS in our fonts.
  • Motion graphics and transitions are timelines it writes in code, rendered at 30 frames a second, then laid over the footage.
  • Illustrations are SVG drawings it writes, so they're original and stay sharp at any size.
  • Sound effects are synthesised in Python and mixed to the same timeline.
  • End screens live as Design artifacts in Claude, so they can be reopened, tweaked and reused.
