Geometry puzzle copy

Some puzzles look hard until you draw one extra line. Then the answer is obvious. Good copy works the same way: find the one fact that makes saying yes feel like common sense, then say it like you would to a mate.

The method

Four moves

People don't weigh up every option. They do what they did last time. Your job is to make the new thing feel obvious and safe, not to win an argument.

1

Find the one extra line

One true fact about the reader or their day that reframes the choice. Not a feature. Not jargon. Something they'd nod at.

SwapEvidence-based driving assessmentsYour teen will learn to drive from someone.
2

Use short, plain words

Old, short English words land in the gut: get, help, start, home. Long Latin words land in a report: obtain, facilitate, commence, residence.

SwapWe facilitate participationWe help you join in
3

Make it move

Verbs do the work, not adjectives. Walk, book, pick, fix, swing. Turn every feature into what it feels like on a Tuesday.

SwapFlexible telehealth sessionsLog on from the couch, no drive
4

Make the next step feel safe

Shrink the first step. Keep what they already do. Name the cost of waiting. Give them a way out.

SwapBook your initial assessmentStart with a free 15 minute call

Test your fact

A good fact: fits in one breath (20 words or fewer), has no jargon, is true even if Estus didn't exist, and changes the question from "should I?" to "which way?". The lab below checks the first two for you.
