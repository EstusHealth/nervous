The hinge

Why this one matters

What breaks

Stopping one thing and starting another. Getting off a game, leaving the house, school pickup, starting work after a meeting.

Why it's a hinge

Days are full of switches. Make the hardest one easier and the whole day has fewer blow-ups.

Nervous system angle

A sudden stop can feel like a threat. Warnings and bridges give the body time to shift. See the nervous system page.

Listen for

Spot it in a welcome call

What clients and families say, and what's usually going on underneath.

You'll hearWhat's usually going on
"Getting him off the game is World War 3."Deep focus plus no save point.
"School pickup is the worst part of the day."After-school restraint collapse meets a transition.
"I can't start work after a meeting."Switching cost and no bridge between tasks.
"Any change of plan ruins the day."Predictability carries the day. Surprise breaks it.

One session

Run the session

Same five moves in every hinge. Map it, find the hinge, build it, test it, leave with something real.

  1. MapThe hardest switch

    Do

    Pick one transition that goes wrong most. Walk through the last time it happened.

    Say it like

    "Which switch in the day goes wrong the most?"

  2. Find the hingeWhere it tips

    Do

    Find the moment it tips: no warning, no finish point, or landing straight into a demand.

    Say it like

    "What happens just before it goes wrong?"

  3. BuildWarn, finish, bridge

    Do

    Build a warning, a finish point, and a bridge activity between the two things.

    Say it like

    "What would a good stopping point look like?"

  4. TestRehearse it

    Do

    Practise the warning and the words. Plan for when it still goes wrong.

    Say it like

    "Let's practise what you'll say at the 10-minute mark."

  5. Leave withThe deliverable

    Do

    A written transition plan for that switch, with the exact words to use.

    Say it like

    "Use it this week. Tell me what changed."

Tools to choose from

Pick what fits. Two or three good ones beat six half-done.

Warnings with time

"10 minutes, then 5, then finish". Visual timers beat verbal reminders.

Find the save point

Stop at a natural end: end of a level, a chapter, a match. Stopping mid-task feels like a loss.

Bridge activity

A short, easy thing between two tasks: a snack, a walk, a song. It softens the switch.

First, then

Show what comes next and what comes after. Predictability lowers the threat.

Transition object

Something to carry from one place to the next. Common for younger kids.

Low-demand landings

After school or work, land in a quiet space before any demand.

Use it in session

Transition script builder

Warn, finish, bridge. Builds the exact words to use. Edit to suit.

Neuroaffirming

Adjust for the person

Same hinge, different way in.

ProfileWhat changes
ADHDTime blindness makes warnings essential. Use visible timers and alarms.
AutisticAttention can tunnel deep into one thing, so pulling out is hard. Use save points and plenty of warning.
PDAOffer the warning as information, not a command. Give choice over the stop point.
Kids and familiesPlan the after-school landing with no demands for the first 30 minutes.
Scope check. Frequent meltdowns with self-injury or aggression need a wider look, including the rest of the team around the client.
