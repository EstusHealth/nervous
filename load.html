The hinge

Why this one matters

What breaks

Too many open loops held in working memory. Things get dropped, everything feels urgent, and nothing gets finished.

Why it's a hinge

Working memory only holds a few things at once. Take the rest out of the head and attention comes back for the work that matters.

Nervous system angle

Open loops keep a low hum of alarm going. Getting them down on paper lets the system settle. See the nervous system page.

Listen for

Spot it in a welcome call

What clients and families say, and what's usually going on underneath.

You'll hearWhat's usually going on
"I've got 47 tabs open. In my browser and my head."Too many open loops.
"I forget things the moment I walk away."Working memory is full, so new things fall out.
"Everything feels urgent."No system to sort, so the brain treats it all as a threat.
"I have lists everywhere."Too many capture points, none trusted.

One session

Run the session

Same five moves in every hinge. Map it, find the hinge, build it, test it, leave with something real.

  1. MapBrain dump

    Do

    Get everything out: tasks, worries, ideas, appointments. Count where they currently capture things.

    Say it like

    "Let's get everything out of your head and onto the page."

  2. Find the hingeThe leak

    Do

    Find the main source of load: too many inboxes, decisions made over and over, or constant switching.

    Say it like

    "Which of these keeps coming back into your head?"

  3. BuildCapture + decide once

    Do

    One trusted inbox. Sort into do, schedule, hand off or drop. Set defaults and templates for repeated decisions.

    Say it like

    "Where will everything go from now on, so your head doesn't have to hold it?"

  4. TestUse it live

    Do

    Add three real items in session. Plan the weekly reset: when, where and how long.

    Say it like

    "When will you empty the inbox each week?"

  5. Leave withThe deliverable

    Do

    One capture system, a sorted list, and a weekly reset in the calendar.

    Say it like

    "Use only this for a week. Notice what tries to escape."

Build with

Tools to choose from

Pick what fits. Two or three good ones beat six half-done.

One trusted inbox

One place for everything. Paper or app, as long as it's always with them.

Do, schedule, hand off, drop

Every item gets one of four decisions. Dropping things is allowed.

Decide once

Defaults, templates and routines for repeat decisions: meals, outfits, replies.

Batch the switching

Group similar tasks. Every switch costs time and focus.

Weekly reset

Same time each week to empty, sort and plan. Short and kind, not perfect.

Clear the visual field

Visual clutter is load too. Clear the desk, close the tabs.

ProfileWhat changes
ADHDMake the inbox fast and always within reach. Body doubling helps the weekly reset.
AutisticClear categories and rules. A set structure reduces decision fatigue.
PDAKeep the system theirs. Avoid language that turns the list into demands.
ParentsShare the load. Make the family calendar and lists visible, not held by one person.
Scope check. Constant overwhelm with anxiety or low mood may need psychology alongside OT.
