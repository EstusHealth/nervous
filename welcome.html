15 minutes, six moves

The call: CLOSER, the Estus way

Hormozi's CLOSER frame, adapted for a neuroaffirming, Ahpra-safe welcome call. Same order, gentler moves: we label the barrier, show the destination and respect the decision. The aim is a clear, easy choice, not a hard sell.

0 min15 min
  1. C

    Clarify why they are here

    2 min
    Say

    "What made you reach out now?"

    "If this call goes well, what would you want to walk away with?"

    Listen for

    The trigger. Something changed: school, work, burnout, a diagnosis.

    Neuroaffirming tweak

    Ask one question at a time. Let silence sit. Typing in chat is fine.

  2. L

    Label the barrier, not the person

    1 min
    Say

    "So the hard part isn't knowing what to do. It's getting started."

    "Have I got that right?"

    Listen for

    A clear yes. If not, ask again. Use their words, not clinical ones.

    Neuroaffirming tweak

    Name the environment or demand, never a flaw. "Mornings ask a lot of your brain", not "you struggle with mornings".

  3. O

    Overview what has been tried

    2 min
    Say

    "What have you tried so far?"

    "What helped, even a little? What made it worse?"

    Listen for

    Past OT, psych, apps, school plans. What they liked about each.

    Neuroaffirming tweak

    No blame for what didn't work. "That approach didn't fit your brain" is enough.

  4. S

    Show the destination

    3 min
    Say

    "What would a good week look like?"

    "Here's how we'd start: [ongoing OT / Small Hinges]. You'd work with [Nik / Nam]."

    Listen for

    A concrete picture in their words: getting to school, finishing work, sleeping.

    Neuroaffirming tweak

    Describe the plan and the person, not results. Never promise outcomes or timeframes.

  5. E

    Explain away concerns

    4 min
    Say

    "What questions do you have?"

    "Is there anything that would make this hard to start?"

    Listen for

    Cost, time, funding, partner, past bad experiences. Use the objections list below.

    Neuroaffirming tweak

    Answer facts plainly. Fees and funding up front, every time.

  6. R

    Respect the decision

    3 min
    Say

    "Would you like me to send the agreement and book the first session, or would you rather have some time? Both are completely fine."

    Listen for

    Their choice, said out loud. Then send the matching template the same day.

    Neuroaffirming tweak

    Offer two clear choices. No deadlines, no scarcity, no "places are filling fast".

Hormozi's version
  • Label them with a problem
  • Overview past pain
  • Sell the vacation
  • Reinforce the decision
Our version
  • Label the barrier, not the person
  • Overview what has been tried, without blame
  • Show the destination, never promise outcomes
  • Respect the decision with two clear choices
The usual worries

Objection handling

An objection is usually a question in disguise. Answer the question, with facts, then hand the choice back.

14 objections

Money"It's too expensive."+

Often meansWorried about cost, or not sure it's worth it.

Try

"Fair question. It's $193.99 an hour, the NDIS rate. Private clients pay the same."

"If cost is the main thing, Small Hinges is 1 to 6 single sessions on one area, so you can start small."

AvoidDiscounting on the spot, or implying it pays for itself.

Money"We're waiting on our NDIS plan."+

Often meansWants to start but funding isn't sorted.

Try

"That's really common. We can hold your referral and book in as soon as the plan is through."

"If you'd like to start sooner, private is an option at the same rate."

AvoidPushing them to self-fund.

Money"We're agency-managed."+

Often meansFunding type we can't take.

Try

"We can only work with self-managed, plan-managed or private. Your support coordinator can talk with you about switching to plan-managed if you'd like to work with us."

AvoidGiving funding advice beyond that.

People"I need to talk to my partner first."+

Often meansShared decision, or a soft no.

Try

"Of course. Would it help if I emailed a short summary you can share?"

"Happy to have a quick call with both of you too."

AvoidAsking them to decide without their partner.

People"My child won't engage. They hate appointments."+

Often meansPast sessions felt like demands. Often PDA.

Try

"That makes sense. We keep demands low and start with what they already love, often a game or a story."

"They don't have to talk, look at the camera or do anything on day one."

AvoidPromising they will definitely engage.

People"Phone calls are hard for me."+

Often meansCommunication preference, not reluctance.

Try

"Thanks for telling me. We can do sessions by chat or text, and we'll stick to it."

AvoidTreating it as a hurdle to get past.

Past"We've tried OT before and it didn't help."+

Often meansBurned before. Needs to know this is different.

Try

"I'm sorry it didn't fit. Can I ask what didn't work?"

"We work with how your brain works, not on making you look less autistic or ADHD. If something isn't working, we change it."

AvoidCriticising the previous clinician.

Past"I don't have a diagnosis" or "I'm not sure I'm autistic enough."+

Often meansFear of not belonging, or of being judged.

Try

"You don't need a diagnosis to work with us privately. We work with what's hard for you now."

"If NDIS funding is the plan, a diagnosis does matter, and we can talk through that."

AvoidDiagnosing or suggesting a diagnosis on the call.

Fit"Is gaming therapy just playing games?"+

Often meansParent sceptical, or worried it adds screen time.

Try

"Good question. The game is the way in, not the goal. We use it to work on planning, regulation and getting along with others, then bring that into real life."

"We only use games if they help. Lots of clients never game in session."

AvoidOverselling the evidence.

Fit"Will telehealth work for us?"+

Often meansUnsure it can be as good as in person.

Try

"For most people, yes. Being at home means less to adjust to. Camera off is fine."

"If it isn't working after a few sessions, we'll talk about it honestly."

AvoidSaying it is always as good as in person.

Fit"I just want an assessment or report."+

Often meansNeeds a document, not ongoing therapy.

Try

"We can do that. Tell me what it's for, like NDIS or school, and who will read it, and I'll explain what's involved and the cost."

AvoidPushing ongoing therapy instead.

Time"I don't have time right now."+

Often meansOverloaded. Maybe burnt out.

Try

"Totally fair. Sessions are by telehealth, so no travel. We can also start with one Small Hinge and stop there."

"Or we can hold your referral and check in later. Whatever works."

AvoidSaying they can't afford not to.

Time"How long will it take?"+

Often meansWants to know the commitment.

Try

"Small Hinges is 1 to 6 sessions. Ongoing OT depends on your goals. We review together regularly, and you can stop any time."

AvoidGiving a promised number of sessions to an outcome.

Time"I need to think about it."+

Often meansGenuine, or an unvoiced concern.

Try

"Of course. Is there anything you're unsure about that I can answer now?"

"I'll email a recap so you don't have to remember it all."

AvoidChasing more than once after the check-in text.

Nothing matches. Try another word, or answer it plainly and add it here next time.

Every call, every message

Guardrails

Always

  • Say fees and funding up front
  • Use their words for their goals
  • Offer chat or text if calls are hard
  • Send the follow-up the same day
  • Refer on when we are not the right fit

Never

  • Promise outcomes or a set number of sessions
  • Use testimonials or client stories
  • Create urgency: "spots filling fast", "today only"
  • Diagnose on the call
  • Chase more than once after the check-in
