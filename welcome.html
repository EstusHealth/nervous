Two minutes, before you dial

Before the call

The call is won before it starts. If you have read the referral, the person does not have to tell their story twice. That is the first thing they notice.

  1. Read the referral. Write down their words for what is hard.
  2. Check funding: self-managed, plan-managed or private. We can't take agency-managed.
  3. Check how they like to talk: video, phone, chat or email.
  4. Check the service they need against who takes what.
  5. Fill once in the templates below, so the follow-up is ready to send.

The first 30 seconds

Adult client

"Hi [name], it's [you] from Estus Health. Thanks for making time."

"We've got 15 minutes. I've read your referral, so you don't have to start from the top. Camera on or off, your call."

Parent or carer

"Hi [name], it's [you] from Estus Health. I've read what you sent about [child]."

"Is [child] joining us, listening in, or is it just us today? All three are fine."

Support coordinator

"Hi [name], [you] from Estus Health. Thanks for the referral."

"Can I check two things first: the plan dates, and how the plan is managed?"

15 minutes, six moves

The call: CLOSER, the Estus way

Hormozi's CLOSER frame, adapted for a neuroaffirming, Ahpra-safe welcome call. Same order, gentler moves: we label the barrier, show the destination and respect the decision. The aim is a clear, easy choice, not a hard sell.

0 min15 min

    Who is on the call changes the moves

    Adult client

    Their goals, their words. Ask how they like to talk before you ask anything else.

    Listen for the moment it breaks: the morning, the inbox, the drive home.

    Parent or carer

    Ask what their child loves before you ask what is hard. Talk about the child as if they are listening. Often they are.

    Ask who else needs to agree before a yes.

    Support coordinator

    Ask for the participant's own goal, not only the plan goal. Check plan dates, funding type and who signs.

    Ask if you can contact the participant directly.

    The usual worries

    Objection handling

    An objection is usually a question in disguise. Answer the question, with facts, then hand the choice back.

    Every call, every message

    Guardrails

    Always

    • Say fees and funding up front
    • Use their words for their goals
    • Offer chat or email if calls are hard
    • Send the follow-up the same day
    • Refer on when we are not the right fit
    • Get consent before you update a referrer

    Never

    • Promise outcomes or a set number of sessions
    • Use testimonials or client stories
    • Create urgency: "spots filling fast", "today only"
    • Diagnose on the call
    • Say or imply the NDIS will fund it
    • Chase more than once after the check-in
