The hinge

Why this one matters

What breaks

Bedtime drifts later, mornings are brutal, and the whole day runs on fumes. Many neurodivergent people run on a later body clock than school and work allow.

Why it's a hinge

Sleep sets the battery for everything else: mood, attention, patience, appetite. Fix the night and the morning, energy and load hinges get easier.

Nervous system angle

Sleep needs the brake on. A wind-down is a planned slide from accelerator to brake, not a light switch at lights out. See the nervous system page.

Listen for

Spot it in a welcome call

What clients and families say, and what's usually going on underneath.

You'll hearWhat's usually going on
"My brain switches on at 11pm."A later body clock, or the first quiet moment of the day.
"It's the only time I get to myself."Bedtime pushed back to claim free time. Common with ADHD.
"I'm fine at night and dead in the morning."Wake time is fighting the body clock.
"I can't sleep if the sheets feel wrong."Sensory environment is part of the problem.

One session

Run the session

Same five moves in every hinge. Map it, find the hinge, build it, test it, leave with something real.

  1. MapThe last 7 nights

    Do

    Walk through a real week: lights out, sleep onset, wake time, naps, caffeine, screens. Use a sleep diary if they did one.

    Say it like

    "Talk me through last night, from dinner to waking up."

  2. Find the hingeOne point of leverage

    Do

    Pick the single change with the most pull. Usually a fixed wake time, or a wind-down that actually starts.

    Say it like

    "If we only changed one thing this fortnight, which would make the biggest difference?"

  3. BuildWindow + wind-down

    Do

    Set a sleep window from wake time backwards. Build a wind-down menu they choose from. Tune the room: light, noise, temperature, textures.

    Say it like

    "What helps your body slow down? Let's pick three you'd actually do."

  4. TestRehearse tonight

    Do

    Walk through tonight step by step. Plan the if-then for a bad night so one miss doesn't end the plan.

    Say it like

    "If you're still awake after 20 minutes, what will you do?"

  5. Leave withThe deliverable

    Do

    One-page wind-down plan and sleep window. One experiment to try for 7 nights.

    Say it like

    "Try this for a week. Then we'll look at what the diary says."

Tools to choose from

Pick what fits. Two or three good ones beat six half-done.

Anchor the wake time

Same wake time 7 days a week matters more than bedtime. It sets the body clock.

Morning light, evening dim

Daylight soon after waking pulls the clock earlier. Dim, warm light in the last hour helps the slide.

Wind-down menu

A short list of calming options they choose from each night. Choice keeps it from feeling like a rule.

Protect "me time" earlier

Revenge bedtime eases when free time is scheduled before the wind-down, not stolen after it.

Screens: content first

What's on the screen often matters more than the light. Swap stimulating content for familiar, low-stakes content.

Sensory sleep space

Textures, weight, noise and temperature. Test one change at a time.

Neuroaffirming

Adjust for the person

Same hinge, different way in.

ProfileWhat changes
ADHDExpect a later clock. Use alarms for the wind-down start, not just the wake. Keep the menu novel.
AutisticMake the routine visual and identical each night. Sensory environment is often the biggest lever.
PDAOffer the plan as options, never orders. Let them own the wind-down. Drop "you have to".
Kids and familiesBuild it with the parent and child together. One shared visual routine on the wall.
Scope check. Snoring with pauses, gasping, or extreme daytime sleepiness: refer to the GP. Long-standing insomnia may need CBT-I from a psychologist. We work on routines, environment and habits.
