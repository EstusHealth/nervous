01 · The system

Only ever sell the next step

The ad isn't there to sell OT. It sells the click. The page sells starting the form. Tap a step to see its job, its number and what moves it.

02 · The maths

Count the cards

Meta tells you most of the numbers. Every number that matters has a % or a $. Change one step and everything after it moves. The defaults are examples only. Put in our real numbers.

Biggest lever: move each step to the top of its healthy range

Same budget, more clients. Whichever bar is longest is the step to work on first.

The scoreboard is profit, not return on ad spend. Spending more pushes ads to colder people, so cost per client rises. That's fine while total profit still goes up. Brandon: 2.5x on a big budget beats 20x on a tiny one. When the numbers are healthy, the easiest win is often to raise the budget, not to keep tinkering.
03 · Principles

Ten lenses to diagnose any ad

Look at an ad, a page or a message through each lens. Most problems show up in one of them.

The Rule of One

From copywriter Michael Masterson. Every ad has one idea, one story, one promise, one emotion, one call to action. No Frankenstein ads. When an ad wins, pull it apart with the same five questions and make more in that direction.

04 · Hooks

Curious vs committed

The first line of text and the first 3 seconds of video do about 80% of the work. Half of viewers are gone by 1.8 seconds. A hook has three jobs: spark curiosity, use committed language so they know it's a service, and filter out the wrong clicks. Brandon calls curious language the most common beginner mistake.

His example: "This is how guys are losing stubborn belly fat" became "This is how the guys I coach are losing stubborn belly fat despite busy 9 to 5s". Fewer clicks, about 4x the conversion, cheaper leads.

    05 · Ad ideas

    One whiteboard, fifty ads

    Since Meta's Andromeda update (mid 2025) the creative does the targeting. Meta reads what you say and show, then finds the people. It rewards lots of genuinely different ads. Start with at least 12 and run them for a month. Every point on this board is an ad.

    Unpack each point with two questions: "Why?" and "Then what happens?" Write the real moment ("shoes off at the door, again, at 8:20"), not the label. If one family said it, a thousand thought it. Nik and Nam hear these on every welcome call, so they're the best source.

      06 · Ad scripts

      Six beats, 25 to 40 seconds

      Every talking ad that works for a local health practice runs the same six beats. We found them in three Exercise Healthcare Australia Reels. Pick a hook from the bank, fill the beats, run the copy check, shoot, then test one thing at a time.

      Three ads, one skeleton

      Tap a block on any bar to see what that part of the ad does. Gaps show the beats each ad skipped.

      Function, not diagnosisChair, floor, carrying. Never "do you have arthritis?"
      Show it in 2 secondsStaff act the problem out on camera.
      "Safe" every timeFear of getting hurt is the main objection.
      Local every timeSuburb name, shopfront, "our clinic here".
      Cheap to makePhone, lapel mic, polo, word-by-word captions.
      CTA says what happensAssessment, phone call, a named person.
      Where EHA leaves money on the table: no funding line, no visible end result, and no named offer. Small Hinges fixes the last one for Estus.

      The six beats

      About 30 seconds, 70 to 100 spoken words. Tap a beat.

      • Captions burned in, centred, 2 to 4 words at a time. Most people watch on mute.
      • A cut or new angle every 3 to 6 seconds.
      • End on a line that loops back into the hook.
      • Add the funding line in beat 5 or 6: "NDIS and private clients welcome".
      Small Hinges offer line for beat 6: "It's called Small Hinges. Pick one area, one session at a time." Test it against a plain "see how our OT works" ending.

      Six scripts

      Shoot each with two or three hook takes so you can test hooks without reshooting the body.

      Write your own

      Fill each beat. The timer assumes about 3 words a second. Aim for the shaded 25 to 40 second zone.

      08 · Must be nice
      Post it free, then pay for the winners
      
  
      Our Must be nice series already does what Brandon asks for: it names the exact moment people live with. Use the reels as free tests. When one gets shared and saved, you've struck oil. Re-film it as an ad with committed language and a call to action. Don't just boost the reel: organic words are written to go wide, ads need to say it's our OT.
      

  
      
    
      1 The lineBrandon: the hook
      "Must be nice to..." is their internal monologue said out loud. Meet them where they're at. It stops the right person because it's oddly specific.
      
    
      2 The turnBrandon: set expectations
      "It's not that it's easy for them. Their setup fits their brain." Answers the story they tell themselves ("it's my fault") before it costs us the click.
      
    
      3 The flipBrandon: away from + tried before
      Inversion: what would guarantee it stays hard? That list is the board's away from column and our FAQ objections.
      
    
      4 The hingeBrandon: one promise
      One small change. In an ad it becomes the one promise: what we'd work on, never a guaranteed result.

        09 · Landing page
        One page, one job
        
  
        A website is a library. A landing page is one book. No menu, no blog, one action. People skim the headline, ignore the long video, see the form, then scroll fast to talk themselves into or out of it. Order sections from biggest idea to smallest detail. Tap a block.
        
  
        
    
        
    
        
  
        

  
        Headline builder: result + feeling
        
  
        
    
        
    
        
    
        
    
        
  
        
  
        Write the clumsy version first, then remix it. Brandon's warning: a tax accountant's "Stress less, sleep well" read like a mattress ad. The result has to be clear. Results beat feelings in his split tests, but you need both.
        

  
        The form: easy first, contact details last
        
  
          
  
          Pixel rule. Only tell Meta a lead happened when the answers qualify. If unsuitable leads count as leads, Meta finds more of them (Brandon's ad account filled with rappers when "music producers" was too vague). And never send diagnosis or health details to Meta: page-level events only.
          

  
          The video (VSL): most important first
          
  
          
  
          
  
          
    
    
          Spend the effort on the first 30 seconds. Don't script the rest word for word: people can tell. Any video beats none, even a 30-second "Hi, I'm Nik, I'll be on your welcome call". It goes on the page, again on the booked page, and in the follow-up emails.

            10 · Show rate
            Keep the excitement up until the call
            
  
            They booked because they want information, but the call might be days away. With no contact, excitement drops and doubts fill the gap with the worst story. So send answers before they ask, and get them to reply. Aim for a 70% show rate. The full speed-to-lead sequence lives in the Nurture Trainer.

            12 · Optimise
            Find the leak, fix one thing
            
  
            Judge on the last 30 days, not today. Day to day is noise (school holidays, petrol prices, Meta news). Give a change at least 7 days or about $1,000 of spend. Steps further down the funnel get fewer events, so they need longer.

            13 · The call
            Therapist, not consultant
            
  
            Brandon's biggest fix for a low close rate: stop teaching on the call. When we explain everything we know, people nod, feel confused, and a confused mind doesn't decide. Be the doctor who asks questions, not the pharmacist who hands out answers. We already run the call with CLOSER in the Welcome Call Playbook. Here's his shape for comparison.
            
  
            
  
            
    
            Objections in three moves
            
      
              
        
            1. Meet them where they are. Lower your energy. "Totally fair, we've covered a lot. What's on your mind?"
              2. 
        
            2. Isolate it. "Is there anything else?" until you have the full list.
              3. 
        
            3. Talk about that one thing only. Feelings need a feelings answer, not more facts. Don't win the argument and lose the family.
              4. 
      
            
      
            "Send me information" = drop the page link in the call chat before it ends, so it's open on their screen. Need to ask a partner? Book the follow-up then and there: "today or tomorrow? morning or afternoon?"
            
    
            
    
            Track it by hand, daily
            
      
            One shared sheet: calls booked, calls attended, starts. Typed in by hand each day, not automated, so you notice a zero today. Know your show rate and start rate for the month within about 3%. Brandon's team found mornings close best and Friday afternoons worst. Watch for "drift": a little off-process each day adds up to a very different call a month later.
