Check 1

The three thresholds

These are signals, not verdicts. They tell us where to look. One miss is a conversation. Two in a row gets its own plan.

Billing attainment90%+

Hours billed against your target. Low usually means gaps in the diary, not effort.

Rebooking rate96%+

Clients who leave a session with the next one booked. The clearest sign the work feels useful to them.

Cancellations6.5%-

Sessions cancelled. High often points to timing, reminders or funding, so look at the pattern first.

  • All metName it as a win. Move on.
  • One missAsk what's behind it. Note it for next check.
  • Two in a rowOwn plan in this 1:1, with an owner and a date.

What each check protects

Why each check

Every line on the card exists because something went wrong without it. Here's what each check is for and what good looks like.

1Numbers

Fast, honest feedback on a job where most feedback is slow. Two weeks is short enough to act on.

Good looks like: all three thresholds met, caseload near capacity, sign-ups counted.

2Notes

Same-day notes and invoices keep records safe and cash moving. The horse emoji tells the team the day is closed off.

Good looks like: zero overdue, NDIS plan end dates checked 90 days ahead.

3Leave

Leave protects the clinician. Clients told early protects trust. Workload and wellbeing get asked every time, not only when things look bad.

Good looks like: next leave on the horizon and visible in every calendar.

4Calendars

Three calendars have to agree: master, PracSuite and the short-notice Google calendar for new leads. When they drift, leads book slots that don't exist.

Good looks like: a new lead could book a real slot today.

5Close

A 1:1 without written actions didn't happen. Wins first, then blockers, then what support is needed.

Good looks like: every action has an owner and a date, sent the same day.

For team leaders

Run it well

A predictable 1:1 is a safe 1:1. Same order, same card, no surprises.

Do

  • Send the card aheadSo numbers can be pulled before the meeting, not during it.
  • Keep the orderNumbers, notes, leave, calendars, close. Every time.
  • Ask before advising"What's behind this one?" before any fix.
  • Start the close with winsName them specifically, the way they like to hear it.

Don't

  • Turn a number into a judgementA miss is a place to look, not a grade.
  • Stack fixesOne plan per metric. Two at most per 1:1.
  • Skip leave and wellbeingEspecially when the numbers are good.
  • Leave actions verbalIf it isn't sent the same day, it didn't happen.
