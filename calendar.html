Systems over willpower

Why the calendar matters

A calendar is a promise you make to your future self. If it's built well, the week runs without heroics.

Lifestyle first

Put the things that keep you well in first: training, family, sleep, leave. Work fills the space that's left, not the other way around.

Burnout is a calendar problem

Most overload is visible in the calendar weeks before it's felt. Back-to-back days, no buffers, no recovery.

Transitions cost energy

Every switch between tasks has a cost. Planned transitions make it smaller. Unplanned ones leak energy all day.

Clients book what you offer

Your calendar sets your caseload. Open only the hours you actually want to work.

Predictable is kind

For you and for clients. Same days, same times, fewer surprises, less load.

We practise what we teach

The same tools we use with clients in Energy, Transitions and Shutdown.

The hidden cost

Transition prompts

A transition prompt is a short plan for the switch: when to start winding down, where to stop, what bridges the gap, and what to do first.

1WarningA heads-up before the next block. 5 to 15 minutes is usually enough.10 minutes until my 2pm client.
2Finish pointA natural place to stop the current task, so it doesn't feel cut off.Finish this paragraph, then save.
3BridgeA short, easy activity between the two. It resets the body.Refill water, two minutes on the treadmill.
4First stepThe very first thing to do in the next block, so starting is easy.Camera on, open last session's summary.
Same idea as Transition Scaffolding for clients: warn, finish, bridge. We use it on ourselves first.

    Calendar work with clients

    The same method works for clients, with adjustments.

    ProfileWhat helps
    ADHDMake time visible. Use warnings on everything. Keep the plan short and novel enough to stay interesting. Body doubling for the weekly reset.
    AutisticSame blocks at the same times each week. Plan changes in advance. Put sensory recovery after social or busy blocks.
    PDAThe calendar is theirs, not a list of orders. Offer options and loose blocks, not strict times. Use declarative prompts.
    FamiliesOne shared visual week on the fridge or phone. Anchors for the whole family first, then each person's blocks.
    Burnout riskBook recovery before anything else. Cap the number of big drains per day. See Energy Management.
