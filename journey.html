Why the route matters

Client experience is therapy

The admin around therapy isn't separate from therapy. A smooth journey keeps people in the work long enough for the work to help.

Smooth journeyClear steps, fast replies, no surprises
TrustThey feel heard and safe
EngagementThey turn up and try things
Steady doseEnough sessions, close enough together
OutcomesGoals move. They tell others.

Outcomes feed trustso the loop repeats

The relationship does the work

Across therapies, the working relationship is one of the most reliable predictors of outcome. Every touchpoint, from first reply to the review report, builds or wears it down.

So: first contact within one business day, plain-language agendas, updates as agreed.

Dropping out ends progress

A client who leaves at week four gets week-four results. Most "lost" clients don't quit therapy. They drift away after a gap nobody noticed.

So: check-ins booked at the start, and turbulence called early.

Predictable lowers the load

For neurodivergent clients, not knowing what happens next costs energy. A clear route frees that energy for the actual work.

So: same route for everyone, sensory and comms preferences captured on day one.

Evidence needs a system

NDIS plan reviews run on evidence. Baselines, ratings and summaries written along the way make the 12-month report easy and strong.

So: baseline at Flight plan, ratings every check-in, summary within a week.

Phase 3: Flight plan

Why we set goals this way

Goals are the steering wheel of therapy. Vague goals give vague therapy and weak NDIS evidence. Goals in the client's own words give them ownership.

Weak

Improve executive functioning.

→
Strong

"Get out the door by 8:15 on school days, 4 out of 5 days, with one reminder."

Weak

Better emotional regulation.

→
Strong

"Use my reset plan after gaming instead of a meltdown, most nights this term."

Weak

Increase independence with daily living.

→
Strong

"Cook two of my own dinners a week without help."

Their wordsOwnership drives effort. A goal they wrote is one they want.
2 to 4 goalsEnough to matter, few enough to remember.
Baseline + targetGAS or COPM ratings show change we can both see.
Linked to NDISPlan goals connect the work to the funding.

How we talk across the journey

Motivational interviewing

MI is a way of talking that helps people find their own reasons to change. Miller and Rollnick's model has four processes. They map straight onto our route.

Listen for change talk

Preparing to change

  • Desire"I want to get to school on time."
  • Ability"I could probably do it with a checklist."
  • Reasons"Then I wouldn't get in trouble."
  • Need"I have to sort this before year 11."

Moving to change

  • Commitment"I'm going to try it this week."
  • Activation"I'm ready to give it a go."
  • Taking steps"I set two alarms last night."
The Estus way: MI, neuroaffirming. MI is never a trick to get compliance. We use it to find the client's own reasons.
  • Ask one question at a time. Leave silence. Chat or typing is fine.
  • Literal questions beat clever ones. Offer two or three options in writing if that helps.
  • Resist the "righting reflex": the urge to fix it for them.
  • Reflect the barrier, not a flaw: "Mornings ask a lot of your brain."

The business side

Why Hormozi keeps talking about continuity

In $100M Money Models, Hormozi sorts offers into four types. Continuity is the one that stacks.

AttractionGets a first yes. For us: the free welcome call.
UpsellMore value straight after. For us: adding a Small Hinges program.
DownsellA smaller yes after a no. For us: a shorter block or fewer sessions.
ContinuityValue that repeats over time. For us: ongoing OT through the year.
Max clients = new per month ÷ % leaving per month

His "hypothetical max". Growth flattens when leavers equal joiners. Cutting churn raises the ceiling more than finding more leads.

Continuity in health: earned

  • Clients stay because the goals keep moving.
  • Q1 to Q4 check-ins booked at the start keep the dose steady.
  • Rebooking and cancellations are the churn levers we track in every Clinician Check.
  • Steady caseloads mean predictable income for clinicians.

Never engineered

  • No lock-in contracts, no pressure to stay.
  • No promised outcomes or fake urgency.
  • Step down and handover are good landings, not losses.
  • NDIS funds goals, not retention. The report must stand on its own.

The Gym Launch lesson. Hormozi sold a big one-off package, and revenue reset to zero every month. Adding a reason to stay on an ongoing program changed everything. Our version is simple: the route itself is the reason to stay. Clients can see where they are going and how far they've come.
